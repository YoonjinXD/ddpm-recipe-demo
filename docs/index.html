Part 1 · Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Ho, Jain & Abbeel, 2020)

A DDPM is three recipes

1. Targetwhat to predictnoise ε, image x0, or v 2. Schedulehow much noise at each steplinear, cosine, sigmoid 3. Samplerhow to generate a sample using those predictionsrandom steps (SDE) or deterministic steps (ODE)

Change one recipe and you get a different model: DDIM, v-prediction, new schedules and even flow matching are new combinations of the same three. Every picture and number below comes from small networks actually trained on MNIST.

An image is just 784 numbers

An MNIST digit is 28 × 28 pixels. DDPM rescales every pixel to [−1, 1] (black = −1, white = +1). Draw each pixel as a bar with its value on top, and the image becomes a landscape. Drag to rotate.

The same picture, as pixels.

1. Targetwhat to predict

Training mixes noise into a clean image and asks the network for something. DDPM asks for the noise ε; you could also ask for the clean image x0, or a mix of both, v. Whatever it predicts, the other two follow by algebra.

3. Samplerhow to generate a sample using those predictions

    
Start from pure Gaussian noise and walk back to an image. DDPM adds fresh noise at every one of its 1000 steps (an SDE);
      DDIM takes the same trained network along a deterministic path (an ODE), so it can skip most steps.

Part 2 · Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023)

    
Flow matching is the same three recipes

    

      1. Targetwhat to predictthe velocity u = ε − x0
      2. Schedulehow to mix image and noisea straight line: nothing to tune
      3. Samplerhow to generate a samplefollow the velocity with a few Euler steps (ODE)
    

    
Diffusion starts from a process that destroys images and learns to undo it. Flow matching starts by drawing a path between noise and data,
      usually a straight line, and learns the velocity to travel along it. Same network, same data, same training budget as Part 1: only the three recipes change.

    
Because the path is drawn by hand, its far end does not even have to be Gaussian noise: flow matching can carry one kind of image into another
      (Liu et al., 2023; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023). Diffusion cannot, since its forward process always ends in noise.

    

  


  
  

    
1. Targetthe velocity

    
Mix the 7 with noise, then ask the network which way every pixel has to move: the velocity u = ε − x0.
      It is a mix of noise and image, like Part 1’s v. Arrows show the network’s answer.

2. Schedulea straight line between image and noise

    
Flow matching has no noise schedule to choose: xt = (1 − t) x0 + t ε slides straight from the image to the noise.
      Compare it with the curved path of Part 1’s cosine schedule: slide t and watch both bury the 7.

    

    

      
      
      
    

    

    

  


  
  

    
3. Samplerfollow the arrows

    
Start from pure noise at t = 1 and repeat one line: move every pixel a small step along the predicted velocity (Euler’s method for an ODE).
      You can also add fresh noise at each step, like DDPM: the sampler is a separate choice from the target and the path.
